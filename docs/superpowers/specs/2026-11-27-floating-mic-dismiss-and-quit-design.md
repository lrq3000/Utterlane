# Floating mic dismissal, feedback, and graceful quit

Approved 2026-11-27. Keep Utterlane’s existing floating mic, drag and pinch gestures, shared dictation panel, and durable processing owners. Adapt the red drop target and save animation from [AudioRecorder](https://github.com/lrq3000/AudioRecorder/tree/659099009252cf4cfc531f4d1074d02bb843b153), under Apache-2.0; do not copy its app-close behavior or inner recorder disc.

- **Hide:** Display a red × during a one-finger drag. Releasing over it hides only the floating mic, keeping the enabled preference and last settled size/position. Neither cancellation nor pinch is a drop. If the floating mic owns a session, Stop ends new capture and the existing speech proceeds through the durable queue, without cancelling unrelated work or closing Utterlane. Hold the old service until the receiving foreground service attaches or the session closes. On a handoff error retain ordinary delivery/recovery. Deliberately reopening Utterlane restores the enabled overlay.
- **Feedback:** The same mic glyph, shape, and size remain during recording; the existing purple–blue background becomes a red gradient without an inner circle. A successful recording stopped with a tap uses AudioRecorder’s approximately three-second rainbow fade and then returns to the current idle gradient. No success animation after cancel, error, hide, or an interrupted animation belonging to an earlier take.
- **Quit:** Add **Stop all jobs and quit** to existing active-work notifications and to the queue screen. Stop live capture; preserve audio, completed results, and resumable progress rather than deleting jobs or waiting for all processing to finish. Stop active queue and archival work at safe boundaries, prevent new work from starting during quit, and close only Utterlane’s tasks after the safe handoffs. Do not force-stop the package or kill the app process; no new idle notification or resident service. Separately configured Android accessibility and monitoring retain their settings.

The full donor service is a poor fit because it couples the drop to closing tasks and owns different recording/save logic. An Utterlane-only redraw would duplicate tested interaction ideas from the requested donor. Adopt the donor’s drop geometry and feedback while keeping Utterlane’s capture, queue and archival lifetime boundaries. Use the existing foreground notifications rather than introducing a second persistent controller.
